## 1. Question

**How do I know if this is true?**

The learner owns the question.

Nobody has to use a family story. Nobody has to answer publicly.

## 2. Story

Jason's worked example:

A claim exists. Before treating it as true, he looks for the artifact that can support it.

Possible receipts include:

- a note
- a message
- a photo
- a calendar entry
- a source document
- a public record
- a commit
- a timestamped receipt

This story is an example, not homework.

## 3. Lesson

A claim and its receipt are different objects.

```text
CLAIM != RECEIPT
RECEIPT != TRUTH
MISSING_RECEIPT != FALSE_CLAIM
UNKNOWN != ZERO
```

The point is to learn what evidence can and cannot establish.

## 4. Game

Pick one harmless claim or object.

Ask:

1. What exactly is being claimed?
2. What receipt would support it?
3. Do we actually have that receipt?
4. What does the receipt prove?
5. What does it not prove?
6. What remains unknown?

Stop if the example becomes private, uncomfortable, or too personal.
