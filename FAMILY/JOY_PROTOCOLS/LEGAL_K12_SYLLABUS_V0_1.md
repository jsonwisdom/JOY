## Permanent classroom grammar

Every grade keeps the same core questions.

```text
SIDE-CAR SUSIE
Did everyone get to speak?
Was the original statement preserved?

CLARITY CLYDE
What rule are we using?
Who made it?
Where does it live?

JUSTICE JUDY
What evidence do we actually have?
What is missing?

MENTAL MICHAEL
How do you know?
What else could explain it?
What would change your conclusion?

GENERAL GARBAGE
Did somebody try to promote garbage into a fact?
```

General Garbage is a recurring mascot for bad reasoning, not a person with real authority. JOKE / FAMILY_STORY unless labeled otherwise.

Students always separate:

```text
OBSERVATION
CLAIM
OPINION
FEELING
JOKE
RULE
EVIDENCE
AUTHORITY
UNKNOWN
CONFLICT
RECEIPT
```

## Kindergarten graduation

The student should be able to say:

```text
I SAW
I THINK
I FEEL
I KNOW
I DON'T KNOW
I CHANGED MY MIND
```

The test is not the right answer. The test is:

```text
PRESERVE THE CLAIM
LABEL THE CLAIM
NAME THE SOURCE
FIND THE RULE
GUARD THE GAP
REPLAY THE REASONING
```

Diploma sentence: I can tell the difference between a claim, a rule, evidence, and a gap.

## Grades 1–5 — Rule literacy

Children should understand that authority has a source.

```text
L = LOCATE the claim
E = EXAMINE the evidence
G = GET the governing rule
A = ASK what authority applies
L = LABEL what remains unknown
```

By grade 5: Is that a classroom rule, school policy, city ordinance, state law, federal law, court decision, or just somebody saying something?

## Grades 6–8 — Evidence + due process

```text
CLAIM ≠ EVIDENCE
DOCUMENT ≠ TRUTH
DOCUMENT ≠ AUTHORITY
OFFICIAL-LOOKING ≠ OFFICIAL
MISSING EVIDENCE ≠ PROOF OF THE OPPOSITE
CONTRADICTION ≠ AUTOMATIC LIE
ACCUSATION ≠ FINDING
```

Evidence ladder:

```text
SOURCE → DOCUMENT → PROVENANCE → RULE → AUTHORITY → DECISION → RECEIPT → REPLAY
```

BOXD classroom hygiene:

```text
1. Preserve the original.
2. Do not overwrite it.
3. Label the source.
4. Compare versions.
5. Record the difference.
6. HOLD when uncertain.
```

No mystical Box. Evidence hygiene only.

## Grades 9–12 — Legal reasoning

Students distinguish constitution, statute, regulation, ordinance, case law, agency guidance, policy, contract, court order, claim, commentary. Authority has hierarchy.

For every problem:

```text
1. ISSUE
2. FACTS
3. SOURCE CLASS
4. GOVERNING RULE
5. AUTHORITY
6. ELEMENTS / TEST
7. EVIDENCE FOR
8. EVIDENCE AGAINST
9. GAPS
10. ANALYSIS
11. CONCLUSION
12. WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE CONCLUSION?
```

Question 12 is mandatory. A student who cannot name what would change the answer does not yet have a finished analysis.

## Humor rail

Clarity Clyde applies K–12.

```text
STORY HUMOR IS ALLOWED
JOKE ≠ FACT
JOKE ≠ TESTIMONY
JOKE ≠ AUTHORITY
JOKE ≠ CHILD QUOTE
JOKE ≠ ACCUSATION
IF HUMOR COULD BE MISTAKEN FOR EVIDENCE: LABEL IT OR REWRITE IT
```

Example labeled JOKE:

```text
General Garbage: Everybody knows it.
Student: Source?
General Garbage: My cousin’s group chat.
Clarity Clyde: HOLD.
```

## Promotion

Students earn skills, not fake legal rank.

```text
SOURCE SCOUT → GAP SPOTTER → CLYDE LABELER → RECEIPT RANGER → REPLAY SCHOLAR → EVIDENCE CAPTAIN → LEGAL REASONING GRADUATE
```

PASS: found primary source, preserved original, identified governing rule, named authority, labeled uncertainty, considered counterevidence, replayed analysis.

HOLD: invented source, invented quote, hidden contradiction, joke used as evidence, opinion presented as law, document presented as authority without checking, missing fact silently filled.

## Senior graduation

A 12th-grade LEGAL graduate can receive a controversial claim and produce claim, source, facts established / disputed / unknown, governing law, authority source, evidence for / against, procedural history, burden of proof, remedy / next process, conclusion, confidence, and what would change the conclusion.

Final sentence: I do not have to know the answer before I know how to test the answer.

```text
KINDERGARTEN: What happened?
1ST GRADE: What rule?
ELEMENTARY: Who made the rule?
MIDDLE SCHOOL: What evidence supports the decision?
HIGH SCHOOL: What authority governs, what process applies, and can another person replay the reasoning?
K–12 FINAL: THINK CLEARLY · SHOW YOUR WORK · PROTECT THE HUMAN · GUARD THE GAP
```

Joy first. Evidence second. Authority must always show its paperwork.
