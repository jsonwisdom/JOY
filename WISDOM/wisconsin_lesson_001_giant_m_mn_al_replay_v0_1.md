## Lesson Thesis

Platteville is the textbook. The M is lesson one. Ziggy asks the question. Gray Baby watches the gap. The child measures reality. Replay preserves how we know.

The lesson does not begin by asking a child to memorize a legend. It begins with a testable mission:

**MISSION: Is the giant M really the biggest?**

## Two-Rail Learning Model

```text

                                  ↓

```

### MN — Observation / Witness

MN records what can be observed, measured, sourced, compared, or left unresolved.

Kid questions:

- How tall is it?
- How wide is it?
- Why is it on a hill?
- What is it made from?
- What could we measure ourselves?
- What other hillside letters would we have to compare it with?

MN rule:

```text
OBSERVATION + MEASUREMENT = EVIDENCE
MISSING_MEASUREMENT = GAP
CONFIDENCE != CORRECTNESS
```

### AL — Context / Meaning

AL preserves why the object matters locally without turning meaning into measurement.

Kid questions:

- Why does Platteville have an M?
- What does mining have to do with the city?
- Why did students build it on this hill?
- Why does the story matter to Platteville families, students, alumni, and local history?

AL rule:

```text
STORY = CONTEXT
CONTEXT != MEASUREMENT
MEANING != CERTIFICATION
```

## Gray Baby Evidence Membrane

Gray Baby does not destroy the story. Gray Baby labels the boundary.

```text
CLAIM = "WORLD'S LARGEST HILLSIDE M"
LOCAL_SOURCE_SUPPORT = DOCUMENTABLE
PHYSICAL_MEASUREMENTS = TESTABLE
GUINNESS_CERTIFICATION = NOT_ESTABLISHED
AUTHORITY = NOT_REQUIRED
```

The educational point is the distinction:

```text
"WORLD'S LARGEST" != "GUINNESS CERTIFIED"
CLAIM != FACT
STORY != EVIDENCE
AL_CONTEXT != MN_EVIDENCE
```
